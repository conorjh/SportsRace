# SportsRace

## Windows build

Install Visual Studio 2026 with the C++ workload and CMake. Run `INSTALL.bat` to initialize the Git submodules and build static SDL3, SDL3_image, SDL3_ttf, and SDL3_mixer libraries for Win32 and x64, in Debug and Release. The script writes the libraries under `external\static`; the Visual Studio project links those archives and does not copy SDL runtime DLLs. SDL_image is configured for the game's image formats, SDL_ttf uses vendored FreeType, and SDL_mixer keeps WAV and MP3 playback enabled.

Open `VisualC\SportsRace.sln` and build the desired configuration. Runtime display settings are available from the main menu's Options screen.

## Career world

Choose **Career** from the main menu to generate the persistent world. Before player-racer creation, a new career simulates at least its first 7 world days (one in-game week) and completes a race for every active AI racer, so the starting roster has real race history. The initial shared roster contains 256 racers, including two player-owned runners and 254 autonomous racers. The replacement policy keeps 254 AI racers active while retired records remain in the database. The world calendar advances at 24x (one world day per real hour), while races, travel, training, and recovery use normal real time.

- The autonomous racers travel between city locations, train and recover, enter scheduled league and open races, earn and spend cash, and carry activity statuses visible on the world map. Open city races prioritize unassigned amateurs and can include idle league racers when they have no imminent fixture, keeping tracks active during season downtime. Tired or injured racers visit the gym to recover before returning to competition. Bankrupt racers retire and are replaced one-for-one to keep the AI population stable.
- The generated country contains regions with named capital cities and cities with different populations; population weights AI assignment. The world map zooms from a city's isometric streets to its region and country, with selectable city/region markers and population/capital details. Every city has four six-runner divisions and an Amateur league; each region has four divisions, and the country has a national league. City Division 1 feeds Regional Division 4, while Regional Division 1 feeds the national league. Regional and national knockout cups run alongside the daily AI-only Amateur Cup, and a home-city Amateur lobby stays open for new racers. Seasons use round-robin fixtures and promotion/relegation. Amateur racers work toward $2,000 in savings before applying for the $1,000 racing license needed to progress into Division 4.
- Career-world regular-season league and open races use six entrants; playoff and cup knockout matches use only their two bracketed competitors. Player-prepared races automatically include owned runners, cycling through larger stables and filling any remaining places with matched AI opponents. Tracks have no per-track concurrent-race cap. Regular league heats batch up to three due fixtures; a short final batch is filled to six available runners so the season cannot stall on a 1v1. The full-field finish awards 6-to-1 league points to scheduled fixture competitors, with each fixture's win/draw/loss retained as a tiebreaker. League races are fee-free; first-place prizes rise from $100 in Amateur to $500 in Division 1, with lower podium prizes scaling accordingly. Mixed-division heats use the highest represented division's prize, while standalone open races retain their entry fee. The career-start history warm-up advances independent race ticks and per-racer activities in parallel; shared results and bankrupt replacements are applied serially. Open a track building to see races currently in progress and observe the existing race at its current simulation time.
- The city starts as an 8x5 isometric map with 13 randomly assigned race tracks for the initial 254 AI racers (100, 200, 250, 500, or 1,000 m); tracks grow at one per 20 AI racers and new districts can be explored by panning. Move the pointer to a map edge to pan and use the mouse wheel over the map to zoom between city, region, and country views. A procedural connected road network with loops is drawn between plots, and civilian cars follow its routes while staying upright, sorting correctly against buildings, and occasionally tooting. Track buildings allow unlimited concurrent races and their live-race list scrolls to show every race. Left-click an enterable venue and hold right-click on a plot for details; use **Career hub** or Escape to return.
- The bookmaker, gym, license office, breeding clinic, player stable, and racer database each have their own screens. Click a displayed racer name to open that runner's profile; while spectating a race, click an entrant to follow them. Completed races open a post-race report with placement, XP, career points, purse net, personal-best and attribute changes; racer profiles include recent race results. Press **F3** for a temporary debug menu with an option to spawn five AI racers into the live career world. Public races and basic wagers are open; breeding requires its license. Slot-machine and roulette minigames remain available at the bookmaker.
- **SportRaceCore** is the shared simulation/race library. **SportsRaceServer** runs the career world headlessly; `SportsRaceServer --ticks 10` runs a bounded ten-step simulation. Use **Save career** in the Career Hub and **Load career** from the main menu; saves use `saves\career.srsave` and format version 3. The loader requires an exact version match and rejects incompatible saves rather than attempting a migration. The initial career simulation must finish before saving. Live race machines are transient: unfinished league fixtures are rescheduled when loaded, while in-progress races are not resumed. Networking and offline progression are not implemented.
- The isometric world map uses selected CC0 sprites from Kenney's Isometric Tiles Buildings, City, and Vehicles packs, plus a synthesized low-volume traffic horn. Source links and licensing are recorded in `media\city\LICENSE.txt`.

Career-world implementation details and prototype rules are recorded in [GDD.md](GDD.md).
